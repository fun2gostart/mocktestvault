1. Solutions (Physical Chemistry • 7 Marks)

Raoult's Law, Colligative Properties, and Abnormal Molar Masses
Law / Property Formula Key Points & Constants
Henry's Law p = K_H · x Higher K_H means lower solubility of gas in liquid. K_H increases with temperature (aquatic life prefers cold water).
Raoult's Law Volatile binary solution: P_total = P_A + P_B = P_A° x_A + P_B° x_B Ideal solutions obey Raoult's law over entire range (ΔH_mix = 0, ΔV_mix = 0, e.g. Benzene + Toluene).
Relative Lowering of VP (P_A° - P_A) / P_A° = x_B = (w_B / M_B) · (M_A / w_A) Colligative property depending on number of solute particles, not nature.
Elevation in Boiling Point ΔT_b = i · K_b · m = i · (1000 · K_b · w_B) / (M_B · w_A) K_b = molal elevation constant (ebullioscopic constant). Water K_b = 0.52 K·kg/mol.
Depression in Freezing Point ΔT_f = i · K_f · m = i · (1000 · K_f · w_B) / (M_B · w_A) K_f = molal depression constant (cryoscopic constant). Water K_f = 1.86 K·kg/mol. Used in ethylene glycol anti-freeze.
Osmotic Pressure Π = i · C R T = i · (w_B R T) / (M_B V) Best colligative property for determining molecular masses of polymers & proteins because measurements are taken at room temperature and molarity is used.
Van 't Hoff Factor (i) & α i = Normal Molar Mass / Abnormal Molar Mass
Dissociation: α = (i - 1) / (n - 1)
Association (dimer): α = (1 - i) / (1 - 1/n)		 For non-electrolyte (glucose, urea): i = 1.
NaCl (i ≈ 2), BaCl₂ (i ≈ 3), K₄[Fe(CN)₆] (i ≈ 5). Acetic acid in benzene associates to dimer (i ≈ 0.5).

2. Electrochemistry (Physical Chemistry • 9 Marks)

Galvanic Cells, Nernst Equation, Conductance & Electrolysis
Concept Formula Notes & Constants
Standard Cell Potential E°_cell = E°_cathode - E°_anode (Reduction potentials) Reaction is spontaneous if E°_cell > 0 (and ΔG° < 0). Anode is oxidation; cathode is reduction (An Ox, Red Cat).
Nernst Equation (at 298 K) E_cell = E°_cell - (0.0591 / n) · log₁₀ Q
Equilibrium: log₁₀ K_c = (n · E°_cell) / 0.0591		 n = number of electrons exchanged in balanced redox reaction. Q = [Products] / [Reactants]. Pure solids and liquids have activity = 1.
Gibbs Energy & Electrical Work ΔG° = -n F E°_cell = -2.303 R T log₁₀ K_c F = Faraday's constant ≈ 96500 C/mol e⁻. Note: E°_cell is an intensive property; ΔG° is extensive.
Conductivity & Molar Conductivity κ = (1/R) · (l/A) = G · G*
Λ_m = (κ × 1000) / Molarity		 κ = conductivity (S·cm⁻¹), G* = cell constant (l/A in cm⁻¹), Λ_m = molar conductivity (S·cm²·mol⁻¹). Conductivity κ decreases on dilution; Molar conductivity Λ_m increases on dilution.
Kohlrausch's Law of Independent Migration Λ°_m = ν₊ λ°₊ + ν₋ λ°₋
Degree of Dissociation: α = Λ_m / Λ°_m
Dissociation Constant: K_a = (c · α²) / (1 - α)		 Used to calculate limiting molar conductivity Λ°_m for weak electrolytes like acetic acid using strong electrolyte data.
Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis m = Z · I · t = (M · I · t) / (n · 96500) 1 Faraday (96500 C) deposits 1 gram-equivalent of any substance (e.g. 1 mol Ag⁺, 0.5 mol Cu²⁺, 1/3 mol Al³⁺).

3. Chemical Kinetics (Physical Chemistry • 7 Marks)

Reaction Rates, Integrated Rate Equations & Arrhenius Theory
Order Integrated Rate Equation Half-Life (t₁/₂) Units of k
Zero Order k = ([R]₀ - [R]) / t ⟹ [R] = -kt + [R]₀ t₁/₂ = [R]₀ / (2k) (proportional to [R]₀) mol·L⁻¹·s⁻¹
First Order k = (2.303 / t) · log₁₀([R]₀ / [R]) t₁/₂ = 0.693 / k (independent of [R]₀!) s⁻¹ or min⁻¹
General n-th Order - t₁/₂ ∝ 1 / [R]₀^(n - 1) (mol·L⁻¹)^(1 - n) · s⁻¹
Arrhenius Equation k = A · e^(-E_a / RT)
log₁₀(k₂ / k₁) = [E_a / (2.303 R)] · [ (T₂ - T₁) / (T₁ T₂) ]		 R = 8.314 J·K⁻¹·mol⁻¹
E_a = activation energy (J/mol). Graph of ln k vs 1/T yields straight line with slope = -E_a/R.

4 & 5. d- & f-Block Elements and Coordination Compounds

Inorganic Chemistry • 14 Marks Combined
Topic / Complex Key Concept / Formula Exam Application
Magnetic Moment (Spin-only) μ = √[ n(n + 2) ] BM n = number of unpaired electrons. Units: Bohr Magneton (BM).
n=1 ⟹ 1.73 BM, n=2 ⟹ 2.83 BM, n=3 ⟹ 3.87 BM, n=4 ⟹ 4.90 BM, n=5 ⟹ 5.92 BM.
Lanthanoid Contraction Steady decrease in atomic and ionic radii from La³⁺ to Lu³⁺ due to poor shielding of 4f electrons. Consequence: Zr and Hf have nearly identical radii and properties; basic character of hydroxides decreases from La(OH)₃ to Lu(OH)₃.
K₂Cr₂O₇ Oxidizing Action (Acidic) Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O (Orange to Green) Oxidizes: Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺; I⁻ → I₂; H₂S → S; Sn²⁺ → Sn⁴⁺.
KMnO₄ Oxidizing Action (Acidic) MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O (Purple to Colorless) Oxidizes: C₂O₄²⁻ (oxalate) → CO₂; Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺; NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻; I⁻ → I₂.
Coordination: VBT Hybridisation [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺: d²sp³ (inner orbital, diamagnetic, low spin)
[CoF₆]³⁻: sp³d² (outer orbital, paramagnetic, high spin)
[Ni(CN)₄]²⁻: dsp² (square planar, diamagnetic)
[NiCl₄]²⁻: sp³ (tetrahedral, paramagnetic, 2 unpaired e⁻) 		Strong field ligands (CN⁻, CO, NH₃) cause electron pairing; Weak field ligands (F⁻, Cl⁻, H₂O) do not pair electrons.
Crystal Field Splitting (CFSE) Octahedral: Δ_o (t₂g lower, e_g higher)
Tetrahedral: Δ_t = (4/9) Δ_o		 Spectrochemical series: I⁻ < Br⁻ < S²⁻ < Cl⁻ < NO₃⁻ < F⁻ < OH⁻ < H₂O < NH₃ < en < NO₂⁻ < CN⁻ < CO. If Δ_o > P, low spin (pairing); if Δ_o < P, high spin.

6. All 28 Canonical Organic Named Reactions Master Suite

Organic Chemistry carries 33 Marks — Master every reagent, starting material, and mechanism
1. Sandmeyer Reaction Haloarenes

Reactant: Benzene diazonium chloride (Ar-N₂⁺Cl⁻)

Reagent: Cu₂Cl₂ / HCl or Cu₂Br₂ / HBr or CuCN / KCN

Ar-N₂⁺Cl⁻ + Cu₂X₂/HX → Ar-X + N₂↑ (X = Cl, Br, CN)

⭐ Iodobenzene is prepared simply by warming diazonium salt with KI without copper catalyst.

2. Gattermann Reaction Haloarenes

Reactant: Benzene diazonium chloride

Reagent: Copper powder (Cu) in presence of HCl or HBr

Ar-N₂⁺Cl⁻ + Cu / HX → Ar-X + N₂ + CuCl

Yield is lower compared to Sandmeyer reaction.

3. Finkelstein Reaction Halogen Exchange

Reactant: Alkyl chloride or bromide (R-Cl or R-Br)

Reagent: Sodium iodide (NaI) in dry acetone

R-X + NaI (dry acetone) → R-I + NaX↓ (X = Cl, Br)

NaCl/NaBr precipitates in dry acetone, driving equilibrium forward (Le Chatelier's principle).

4. Swarts Reaction Fluoroalkanes

Reactant: Alkyl chloride or bromide

Reagent: Heavy metallic fluoride (AgF, Hg₂F₂, CoF₃, or SbF₃)

R-Br + AgF → R-F + AgBr↓

The only convenient laboratory method to prepare alkyl fluorides.

5. Wurtz Reaction Hydrocarbons

Reactant: 2 molecules of Alkyl halide (R-X)

Reagent: Sodium metal (Na) in dry ether

2 R-X + 2 Na (dry ether) → R-R (symmetrical alkane) + 2 NaX

Methane cannot be prepared; only good for symmetrical alkanes with even number of carbons.

6. Fittig & Wurtz-Fittig Reactions Biaryls

Wurtz-Fittig: Ar-X + R-X + 2Na (dry ether) → Ar-R + 2NaX (Alkylarene)

Fittig: 2 Ar-X + 2Na (dry ether) → Ar-Ar (Diphenyl) + 2NaX

2 C₆H₅Cl + 2Na (dry ether) → C₆H₅-C₆H₅ (Biphenyl) + 2NaCl
7. Kolbe's Reaction Phenols

Reactant: Phenol (converted to Sodium phenoxide)

Reagents: 1. CO₂ (400 K, 4-7 atm) 2. H⁺ acidification

Phenol + NaOH → C₆H₅ONa + CO₂/H⁺ → 2-Hydroxybenzoic acid (Salicylic acid)

Salicylic acid is acetylated with (CH₃CO)₂O to manufacture Aspirin.

8. Reimer-Tiemann Reaction Phenols

Reactant: Phenol

Reagents: Chloroform (CHCl₃) + aqueous NaOH, followed by H⁺

Phenol + CHCl₃ + 3NaOH → Salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde)

Electrophile is Dichlorocarbene (:CCl₂). If CCl₄ is used, Salicylic acid is formed.

9. Williamson Ether Synthesis Ethers

Reactant: Alkyl halide + Sodium alkoxide (R'-ONa)

R-X + R'-ONa → R-O-R' (Ether) + NaX

⭐ Follows S_N2 mechanism! Alkyl halide R-X must be 1° only. If 3° alkyl halide is used, elimination dominates to give alkene!

10. Rosenmund Reduction Aldehydes

Reactant: Acyl chloride (Acid chloride R-COCl)

Reagents: H₂ over Pd poisoned with BaSO₄ (and sulfur/quinoline)

R-COCl + H₂ (Pd-BaSO₄) → R-CHO + HCl

BaSO₄ acts as catalyst poison to prevent further reduction of aldehyde to alcohol.

11. Stephen Reaction Nitriles

Reactant: Nitrile (R-CN)

Reagents: SnCl₂ + HCl, followed by boiling with H₂O

R-CN + SnCl₂/HCl → R-CH=NH·HCl (Imine) + H₃O⁺ → R-CHO
12. Etard Reaction Aromatic Aldehydes

Reactant: Toluene (C₆H₅CH₃)

Reagents: Chromyl chloride (CrO₂Cl₂) in CS₂, followed by H₃O⁺

C₆H₅CH₃ + CrO₂Cl₂ (CS₂) → Brown Chromium Complex + H₃O⁺ → C₆H₅CHO (Benzaldehyde)
13. Gattermann-Koch Reaction Formylation

Reactant: Benzene

Reagents: CO + HCl in presence of anhydrous AlCl₃ / CuCl

C₆H₆ + CO + HCl (anh. AlCl₃) → C₆H₅CHO (Benzaldehyde) + HCl
14. Clemmensen & Wolff-Kishner Carbonyl to CH₂

Clemmensen (Acidic): >C=O + Zn-Hg / conc. HCl → >CH₂ + H₂O

Wolff-Kishner (Basic): >C=O + NH₂NH₂ (Hydrazine) → Hydrazone + KOH/ethylene glycol (heat) → >CH₂ + N₂↑

Use Clemmensen for base-sensitive compounds; use Wolff-Kishner for acid-sensitive compounds.

15. Aldol Condensation Enolates

Requirement: Aldehyde or ketone having at least one α-hydrogen

Reagent: Dilute alkali (dil. NaOH or Ba(OH)₂), followed by heat

2 CH₃CHO (dil. NaOH) → CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CHO (Aldol) + Δ → CH₃CH=CHCHO (But-2-enal)

Cross-aldol between two different aldehydes with α-H yields a mixture of 4 products.

16. Cannizzaro Reaction Disproportionation

Requirement: Aldehydes having NO α-hydrogen (HCHO, C₆H₅CHO, (CH₃)₃CCHO)

Reagent: Concentrated alkali (50% conc. KOH or NaOH)

2 HCHO + conc. NaOH → CH₃OH (reduced) + HCOONa (oxidized)

Self-redox: one molecule reduces to alcohol, other oxidizes to carboxylic acid salt.

17. Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) Carboxylic Acids

Requirement: Carboxylic acids having α-hydrogen

Reagents: X₂ (Cl₂ or Br₂) in presence of Red Phosphorus, then H₂O

R-CH₂-COOH + Br₂/Red P → R-CH(Br)-COOH (α-Bromocarboxylic acid)
18. Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis Amines

Purpose: Preparation of pure Primary Aliphatic Amines (1° R-NH₂)

Steps: Phthalimide + ethanolic KOH → Potassium phthalimide + R-X → N-Alkylphthalimide + aq. NaOH → 1° Amine + Phthalate ion

⭐ Aromatic 1° amines (Aniline) CANNOT be prepared because aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution (S_N2) with phthalimide anion.

19. Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation Step-Down Synthesis

Reactant: Acid Amide (R-CONH₂)

Reagents: Bromine (Br₂) + ethanolic or aqueous NaOH / KOH

R-CONH₂ + Br₂ + 4 NaOH → R-NH₂ (1° amine) + Na₂CO₃ + 2 NaBr + 2 H₂O

Crucial step-down reaction: Product amine contains one carbon less than starting amide!

20. Carbylamine Reaction (Isocyanide Test) 1° Amine Test

Reactant: 1° Aliphatic or Aromatic Amine (R-NH₂ or Ar-NH₂)

Reagents: Chloroform (CHCl₃) + alcoholic KOH, warmed

R-NH₂ + CHCl₃ + 3 KOH (alc.) → R-NC (Isocyanide) + 3 KCl + 3 H₂O

Gives an extremely foul, obnoxious smell of isocyanide/carbylamine. 2° and 3° amines DO NOT show this test.

21. Diazotisation & Azo Coupling Azo Dyes

Diazotisation: C₆H₅NH₂ + NaNO₂ + 2 HCl (273–278 K) → C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + NaCl + 2H₂O

Coupling with Phenol (pH 9-10): C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + Phenol → p-Hydroxyazobenzene (Orange Dye)

Coupling with Aniline (pH 4-5): C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + Aniline → p-Aminoazobenzene (Yellow Dye)

22. Friedel-Crafts Alkylation & Acylation Electrophilic Substitution

Alkylation: Ar-H + R-Cl (anh. AlCl₃) → Ar-R + HCl

Acylation: Ar-H + RCOCl (anh. AlCl₃) → Ar-COR (Acetophenone / Benzophenone)

⭐ Aniline does NOT undergo Friedel-Crafts reaction because basic -NH₂ group forms an insoluble salt with Lewis acid AlCl₃ catalyst!

7. Chemical Distinction Tests Master Matrix

Mandatory 2-mark & 3-mark questions in CBSE Board Papers Section B & C
Pair of Compounds Chemical Test / Reagent Observation for Compound A Observation for Compound B
1°, 2°, and 3° Alcohols Lucas Test
(Conc. HCl + anhyd. ZnCl₂)		 3° Alcohol: Turbidity appears immediately.
2° Alcohol: Turbidity appears within 5 minutes.		 1° Alcohol: Solution remains clear at room temperature (turbidity only on heating).
Methanol vs Ethanol Iodoform Test
(I₂ + NaOH, warm)		 Methanol: No yellow precipitate (does not have CH₃-CH(OH)- group). Ethanol: Yellow crystalline precipitate of Iodoform (CHI₃) with antiseptic smell.
Phenol vs Alcohol (e.g. Ethanol) Neutral FeCl₃ Test Phenol: Violet / Purple coloration (forms iron-phenoxide complex). Ethanol: No violet color change.
Phenol vs Benzoic Acid Sodium Bicarbonate Test
(Sat. NaHCO₃ solution)		 Phenol: No effervescence (too weak an acid to decompose NaHCO₃). Benzoic Acid: Brisk effervescence with evolution of colorless CO₂ gas.
Aldehyde vs Ketone Tollens' Test (Silver Mirror)
[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ OH⁻		 Aldehyde: Bright silver mirror on inner test tube walls. Ketone: No silver mirror formed.
Acetaldehyde vs Benzaldehyde Fehling's Solution or Iodoform Test Acetaldehyde: Red precipitate of Cu₂O with Fehling's, and yellow CHI₃ ppt with I₂/NaOH. Benzaldehyde: No red Cu₂O ppt (aromatic aldehydes do not reduce Fehling's) and no iodoform ppt.
Pentan-2-one vs Pentan-3-one Iodoform Test
(I₂ + NaOH, warm)		 Pentan-2-one: Bright yellow precipitate of CHI₃ (contains methyl ketone group CH₃-C=O). Pentan-3-one: No yellow precipitate (no methyl ketone group).
1° Amine vs 2° / 3° Amine Carbylamine Test
(CHCl₃ + alc. KOH, warm)		 1° Amine: Extremely foul, offensive smell of isocyanide. 2° and 3° Amines: No offensive smell (negative test).
Aliphatic 1° Amine vs Aniline Azo Dye Test
(NaNO₂ + HCl at 0-5°C, then β-naphthol)		 Ethylamine: Rapid effervescence of N₂ gas with no dye formation. Aniline: Brilliant orange-red azo dye precipitate formed.
1°, 2°, and 3° Amines Hinsberg Test
(Benzenesulfonyl chloride C₆H₅SO₂Cl + aq. KOH)		 1° Amine: Precipitate forms which is soluble in alkali (KOH) due to acidic hydrogen on nitrogen. 2° Amine: Precipitate forms which is insoluble in KOH.
3° Amine: Does not react at all.

8. Biomolecules (Organic Chemistry • 7 Marks)

Carbohydrates, Amino Acids, Proteins & Nucleic Acids
Substance / Reaction Reagents & Equations Structural Deduction
Glucose + HI (heat) Glucose + HI (Δ) → n-Hexane Proves that all 6 carbon atoms in glucose are linked in a straight unbranched chain.
Glucose + NH₂OH / HCN Forms Oxime with hydroxylamine; forms Cyanohydrin with HCN. Confirms the presence of a carbonyl group (>C=O).
Glucose + Br₂ water Glucose + Br₂ water → Gluconic acid (Mild oxidation) Proves that the carbonyl group is an aldehyde group (-CHO), not a ketone.
Glucose + (CH₃CO)₂O Forms Glucose Pentaacetate with acetic anhydride. Confirms the presence of 5 hydroxyl groups (-OH) attached to 5 different carbon atoms.
Glucose + HNO₃ Glucose + HNO₃ → Saccharic acid (Dicarboxylic acid) Proves that the terminal sixth carbon is a primary alcohol group (-CH₂OH).
Peptide Linkage -CO-NH- bond formed between -COOH of one amino acid and -NH₂ of another with elimination of H₂O. Proteins are polymers of α-amino acids linked by peptide bonds.
Protein Denaturation Physical (heat) or chemical (pH change) disruption of secondary and tertiary structures. Hydrogen bonds break; protein coagulates. Primary structure remains intact (peptide bonds do not break!). Example: coagulation of egg white on boiling, curdling of milk.
DNA vs RNA DNA: β-D-2-deoxyribose sugar; Nitrogen bases: Adenine, Guanine, Cytosine, Thymine; Double helix.
RNA: β-D-ribose sugar; Nitrogen bases: Adenine, Guanine, Cytosine, Uracil; Single strand. 		Base pairing in DNA: A=T (2 H-bonds) and G≡C (3 H-bonds).
