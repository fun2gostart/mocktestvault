Chapter 1: Electric Charges and Fields

Unit I • Electrostatics (Chapters 1 & 2 combine for 16 Marks)
Practice Board PYQs →
Concept / Law Mathematical Formula SI Units & Key Variables
Coulomb's Law F = (1 / 4πε₀) · (|q₁ q₂| / r²)
Vector: F₁₂ = (1 / 4πε₀) · (q₁ q₂ / r²) · r̂₂₁		 N (Newton)
1/(4πε₀) = 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²
ε₀ = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² C²/N·m²
Electric Dipole Moment p = q · 2a (Directed -q to +q) C·m (Coulomb-meter)
Vector quantity pointing from negative to positive charge.
Dipole Field: Axial Point E_axial = (1 / 4πε₀) · (2pr / (r² - a²)²)
For r >> a: E_axial ≈ (1 / 4πε₀) · (2p / r³)		 N/C or V/m
Field points in the direction of dipole moment p.
Dipole Field: Equatorial Point E_eq = (1 / 4πε₀) · (p / (r² + a²)^(3/2))
For r >> a: E_eq ≈ (1 / 4πε₀) · (p / r³)		 N/C
Note: E_axial = 2 · E_eq. Equatorial field points anti-parallel to p.
Torque & Potential Energy on Dipole τ = p × E = p E sin θ
U = -p · E = -p E cos θ		 Torque: N·m; Energy: J (Joules)
Stable equilibrium at θ = 0°; Unstable at θ = 180°.
Gauss's Law & Flux Φ_E = ∮ E · dA = q_encl / ε₀ N·m²/C or V·m
Flux depends only on enclosed charge, independent of shape/size.
Field: Infinite Straight Wire E = λ / (2πε₀ r) λ = linear charge density (C/m). Field varies as 1/r.
Field: Infinite Plane Sheet E = σ / (2ε₀) σ = surface charge density (C/m²). Independent of distance r!
Field: Thin Spherical Shell Inside (r < R): E = 0
Outside (r ≥ R): E = (1 / 4πε₀) · (q / r²)		 At surface: E_max = σ / ε₀. Inside a conductor or conducting shell, field is strictly zero.
📌 Key 5-Mark Derivation Blueprint: Gauss's Law to Plane Sheet

1. Consider a thin infinite plane sheet with uniform surface charge density σ. Symmetry dictates E is perpendicular to the sheet pointing outward on both sides.
2. Choose a cylindrical Gaussian surface of cross-sectional area A and length 2r cutting symmetrically through the sheet.
3. Flux through curved cylindrical surface = ∮ E · dA = E · A · cos 90° = 0.
4. Flux through two end circular caps = E · A + E · A = 2EA.
5. Enclosed charge = q_encl = σ · A.
6. By Gauss's Law: 2EA = (σ · A) / ε₀ ⟹ E = σ / (2ε₀) (Independent of r).

⚠️ Examiner Pitfall Alert:
In dipole derivations, students often confuse E_axial and E_eq. Remember that E_axial = 2 · E_eq at large distances (r >> a), and E_eq points in the direction OPPOSITE to dipole moment vector p.

Chapter 2: Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance

Unit I • Conservative Fields, Dielectrics & Stored Energy
Concept Formula Remarks
Potential due to Point Charge V = (1 / 4πε₀) · (q / r) V (Volt = J/C). Scalar quantity. V varies as 1/r.
Potential due to Dipole V = (1 / 4πε₀) · (p cos θ / r²) Axial (θ = 0°): V = kp/r².
Equatorial (θ = 90°): V = 0 everywhere!
Field-Potential Relation E = -dV / dr Field points in the direction in which potential decreases steepest. Equipotential surfaces are always perpendicular to electric field lines.
Parallel Plate Capacitor Vacuum: C₀ = ε₀ A / d
With Dielectric: C = K · C₀ = ε_r ε₀ A / d		 F (Farad). C depends only on geometric factors (A, d) and permittivity of medium, NOT on Q or V.
Capacitor with Slab (Thickness t) Dielectric Slab: C = ε₀ A / (d - t + t/K)
Conducting Slab (K = ∞): C = ε₀ A / (d - t)		 Always increases capacitance because effective separation between plates decreases.
Energy Stored in Capacitor U = (1/2) C V² = (1/2) Q V = Q² / (2C)
Energy Density: u = (1/2) ε₀ E²		 Energy is stored in the electrostatic field between the plates. Energy density unit: J/m³.
Combination of Capacitors Series: 1/C_s = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + ... (Q same)
Parallel: C_p = C₁ + C₂ + ... (V same)		 Series divides voltage; parallel divides charge.
Common Potential & Energy Loss V_common = (C₁V₁ + C₂V₂) / (C₁ + C₂)
ΔU = (1/2) [C₁C₂ / (C₁ + C₂)] · (V₁ - V₂)²		 Energy loss ΔU is always positive and dissipates as heat and EM radiation in connecting wires.

Unit II • 7 Marks in Board Blueprints
Parameter / Law Formula Key Physical Constants
Drift Velocity & Current v_d = -e E τ / m
I = n e A v_d		 τ = relaxation time (s), m = electron mass (9.1 × 10⁻³¹ kg), e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, n = electron density (m⁻³).
Current Density & Ohm's Law (Microscopic) j = I / A = σ E
ρ = 1/σ = m / (n e² τ)		 j = current density (A/m²), σ = conductivity (S/m or Ω⁻¹m⁻¹), ρ = resistivity (Ω·m).
Temperature Dependence of Resistance R_T = R₀ (1 + α ΔT)
ρ_T = ρ₀ (1 + α ΔT)		 α = temperature coefficient of resistance (K⁻¹ or °C⁻¹).
Metals: α > 0; Semiconductors/Insulators: α < 0.
Cell EMF, Terminal PD & Internal Resistance Discharging: V = E - I r
Charging: V = E + I r
Internal Resistance: r = R (E/V - 1)		 E = open-circuit EMF (V), V = terminal potential difference (V), r = internal resistance (Ω). Maximum current I_max = E/r when external R = 0.
Kirchhoff's Laws KCL (Junction Rule): Σ I = 0
KVL (Loop Rule): Σ ΔV = Σ E - Σ IR = 0		 KCL is based on Conservation of Charge.
KVL is based on Conservation of Energy.
Balanced Wheatstone Bridge P / Q = R / S (when I_g = 0) When bridge is balanced, no current flows through galvanometer; interchanging battery and galvanometer does not affect balance condition!

Chapters 4 & 5: Moving Charges, Magnetism & Matter

Unit III • 8 Marks
Law / Configuration Formula Notes
Lorentz Force F = q(E + v × B)
Magnetic: F_m = q(v × B) = q v B sin θ		 Magnetic force does ZERO work because F_m is always perpendicular to velocity v (power P = F · v = 0). Kinetic energy remains constant.
Biot-Savart Law dB = (μ₀ / 4π) · (I dl × r̂ / r²) μ₀ / 4π = 10⁻⁷ T·m/A. Valid for steady currents in conductors.
Field: Axis of Circular Loop B = (μ₀ I R²) / [2(R² + x²)^(3/2)]
At Center (x = 0): B_center = μ₀ I / (2R)		 For N turns, multiply by N. At large distances (x >> R): B = (μ₀ / 4π) · (2m / x³) where magnetic dipole moment m = I A = I (π R²).
Ampere's Circuital Law ∮ B · dl = μ₀ I_encl
Long straight wire: B = μ₀ I / (2π r)
Solenoid (inside): B = μ₀ n I		 n = N / L (turns per unit length). At ends of solenoid, field drops to B_end = (1/2) μ₀ n I.
Force between Parallel Currents F / L = (μ₀ I₁ I₂) / (2π d) Parallel currents in SAME direction ATTRACT; antiparallel currents REPEL. Defines 1 Ampere (F/L = 2 × 10⁻⁷ N/m at d = 1 m).
Moving Coil Galvanometer Deflection: θ = (N A B / C) · I
Current Sensitivity: I_s = θ / I = NAB / C
Voltage Sensitivity: V_s = θ / V = NAB / (C R_g)		 Radial magnetic field produced using concave magnetic poles and soft iron core ensures θ is linearly proportional to I (sin 90° = 1 always).
Galvanometer Conversions To Ammeter: Shunt S = I_g R_g / (I - I_g) in parallel.
To Voltmeter: Resistor R = (V / I_g) - R_g in series.		 Ideal Ammeter has ZERO resistance. Ideal Voltmeter has INFINITE resistance.

Chapters 6 & 7: Electromagnetic Induction & Alternating Current

Unit IV • 8 Marks
Concept Formula Key Variables
Faraday's & Lenz's Law E = -dΦ_B / dt = -N · d(B A cos θ) / dt Negative sign represents Lenz's Law, which is a consequence of Conservation of Energy.
Motional EMF Translational: E = B v l
Rotational: E = (1/2) B ω l²		 v = velocity perpendicular to B and length l. Rotational rod rotated about one end with angular speed ω.
Self & Mutual Inductance Φ = L I ⟹ E = -L (dI / dt)
Solenoid: L = μ₀ N² A / l
Mutual: M = μ₀ N₁ N₂ A / l		 Unit: H (Henry). Stored magnetic energy: U_B = (1/2) L I². Energy density u_B = B² / (2μ₀).
AC Quantities (RMS Values) I_rms = I₀ / √2 ≈ 0.707 I₀
V_rms = V₀ / √2 ≈ 0.707 V₀		 Household supply (220 V AC) has peak voltage V₀ = 220 × √2 ≈ 311 V, making AC more lethal than DC of same nominal voltage!
Reactance & Series LCR Impedance X_L = ω L = 2π f L
X_C = 1 / (ω C) = 1 / (2π f C)
Z = √[ R² + (X_L - X_C)² ]		 Units: Ω (Ohm).
Phase angle: tan φ = (X_L - X_C) / R.
Pure inductor: current lags voltage by 90°. Pure capacitor: current leads by 90°.
Resonance & Quality Factor (Q) ω₀ = 1 / √(L C) ⟹ f₀ = 1 / [2π √(L C)]
Q = (ω₀ L) / R = (1 / R) · √(L / C)		 At resonance: X_L = X_C, Z_min = R, current I_max = V/R, power factor cos φ = 1. High Q factor means sharp selectivity (essential in radio tuning).
AC Power & Power Factor P_avg = V_rms · I_rms · cos φ Power factor = cos φ = R / Z. In pure inductive or capacitive circuit, cos 90° = 0, so average power dissipated = 0 (Wattless current).
Ideal Transformer Equations V_s / V_p = N_s / N_p = I_p / I_s = k k > 1 (Step-up: V_s > V_p, I_s < I_p); k < 1 (Step-down). Core laminated to minimize eddy current losses.

Chapters 9 & 10: Ray & Wave Optics

Unit VI • Optics is the heaviest unit with 18 Marks!
Optical Formula Mathematical Expression Sign Convention & Crucial Notes
Mirror & Lens Formulas Mirror: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f (m = -v/u)
Lens: 1/v - 1/u = 1/f (m = +v/u)		 Cartesian convention: light travels left to right. Real objects have u < 0. Convex lens f > 0; Concave lens f < 0.
Lens Maker's Formula 1/f = (μ₂/μ₁ - 1) · [1/R₁ - 1/R₂] μ₂ = lens medium, μ₁ = surrounding medium. If immersed in liquid with μ₁ > μ₂, a convex lens behaves as a concave lens!
Refraction at Spherical Surface μ₂ / v - μ₁ / u = (μ₂ - μ₁) / R Fundamental derivation stepping stone to Lens Maker's formula.
Prism Formula μ = sin[(A + D_m)/2] / sin(A/2) A = angle of prism, D_m = angle of minimum deviation. At minimum deviation: i = e, r₁ = r₂ = A/2, and refracted ray is parallel to prism base.
Compound Microscope Magnification Normal Adjustment (image at ∞): m = -(v_o / u_o) · (D / f_e)
Near Point (image at D): m = -(v_o / u_o) · (1 + D / f_e)		 D = least distance of distinct vision (25 cm). Both objective and eyepiece must have small focal lengths (f_o < f_e < D) with f_o very small for large m. Tube length L ≈ v_o + f_e.
Astronomical Telescope Magnification Normal Adjustment: m = -f_o / f_e (L = f_o + f_e)
Near Point: m = -(f_o / f_e) · (1 + f_e / D)		 Objective must have large focal length and large aperture (for high light-gathering power and resolution); eyepiece must have small focal length.
Wave Optics: YDSE Fringe Width β = λ D / d
Bright Fringe: y_n = n λ D / d (Path diff = nλ)
Dark Fringe: y_n = (n - 1/2) λ D / d		 λ = wavelength, D = distance from slits to screen, d = slit separation. If whole apparatus is immersed in medium of index μ: β' = β / μ (fringes shrink).
Single Slit Diffraction Minima Condition: a sin θ = n λ (n = 1, 2, ...)
Central Maximum Width: 2β₀ = 2 λ D / a		 a = slit width. Note key difference from YDSE: In diffraction, a sin θ = nλ gives MINIMA, whereas in YDSE d sin θ = nλ gives MAXIMA! Central maximum is twice as wide as secondary maxima.

Chapters 11–14: Modern Physics & Semiconductor Electronics

Units VII, VIII, IX • 20 Marks Combined
Phenomenon Formula Key Constant & Interpretation
Einstein's Photoelectric Equation K_max = h ν - φ₀ = e V₀
h ν = h ν₀ + (1/2) m v_max²		 h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, φ₀ = work function (eV), V₀ = stopping potential (V). Slope of V₀ vs ν graph is h/e (universal constant, independent of metal).
de Broglie Wavelength λ = h / p = h / (m v) = h / √(2m E)
Accelerated by V: λ = 1.227 / √V nm		 For thermal neutron at T Kelvin: λ = h / √(3m k_B T).
Bohr Model Formulas (Hydrogen-like) Radius: r_n = 0.529 · (n² / Z) Å
Velocity: v_n = 2.18 × 10⁶ · (Z / n) m/s
Energy: E_n = -13.6 · (Z² / n²) eV		 Kinetic energy K = -E_n; Potential energy U = 2E_n = -2K. Negative total energy signifies that the electron is bound to the nucleus.
Rydberg Formula & Spectral Series 1 / λ = R · Z² · [1/n₁² - 1/n₂²] R = 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹.
Lyman (n₁=1, UV); Balmer (n₁=2, Visible); Paschen (n₁=3, IR); Brackett (n₁=4, IR); Pfund (n₁=5, IR).
Nuclear Radius, Density & Q-Value R = R₀ · A^(1/3) (R₀ ≈ 1.2 fm)
Nuclear Density: ρ_nuc ≈ 2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³
Q = [m_reactants - m_products] · 931.5 MeV		 Nuclear density is INDEPENDENT of mass number A (constant for all nuclei). Q > 0 = Exothermic (fission/fusion); Q < 0 = Endothermic.
Semiconductor Mass Action Law n_e · n_h = n_i²
Conductivity: σ = e (n_e μ_e + n_h μ_h)		 Intrinsic: n_e = n_h = n_i. n-type: n_e ≈ N_D >> n_h. p-type: n_h ≈ N_A >> n_e. Depletion layer width decreases in forward bias and increases in reverse bias.

⭐ Top 15 Mandatory 5-Mark Derivations Master Checklist

These 15 proofs appear every single year in CBSE Class 12 Board Exams Section D & E

1. Axial & Equatorial Electric Dipole Field

Resolve components using Coulomb's law. Axial: E = 2kp/r³ along p; Equatorial: E = kp/r³ anti-parallel to p.

2. Gauss's Law: Field of Infinite Line Charge

Construct co-axial cylindrical Gaussian surface. Flux = E(2πrl) = q_encl/ε₀ = λl/ε₀ ⟹ E = λ / (2πε₀r).

3. Parallel Plate Capacitor with Dielectric Slab

V = E₀(d - t) + E_slab(t) = E₀(d - t + t/K). Substitute E₀ = σ/ε₀ = Q/(Aε₀) ⟹ C = ε₀A / (d - t + t/K).

4. Microscopic Ohm's Law (Drift Velocity)

Apply F = -eE = ma ⟹ v_d = -eEτ/m. Current I = neAv_d ⟹ I = (ne²Aτ/m)E ⟹ j = σE and ρ = m/(ne²τ).

5. Magnetic Field on Axis of Circular Loop

Biot-Savart dB = (μ₀/4π) · (Idl/r²). Diametrically opposite perpendicular components cancel; axial dB sin θ integrate to B = μ₀IR² / [2(R² + x²)^(3/2)].

6. Force Between Two Long Parallel Conductors

Field of wire 1 at wire 2: B₁ = μ₀I₁ / (2πd). Force on length L: F₂₁ = I₂L B₁ sin 90° = (μ₀I₁I₂L) / (2πd). Defines standard SI Ampere.

7. Moving Coil Galvanometer Torque Equation

Deflecting torque τ_d = NIAB sin θ = NIAB (radial field). Restoring torque τ_r = Cθ. Equating gives I = (C / NAB)θ.

8. Motional EMF in Sliding Conductor

Magnetic flux enclosed Φ = B · A = B l x. Rate of flux change dΦ/dt = B l (dx/dt) = B l v. By Faraday's Law, E = -dΦ/dt = Bvl.

9. Series LCR Impedance by Phasor Diagram

Phasors: V_R = IR in phase, V_L = IX_L leads by π/2, V_C = IX_C lags by π/2. Resultant V² = V_R² + (V_L - V_C)² ⟹ Z = √[R² + (X_L - X_C)²].

10. Refraction at Spherical Surface

Snell's law for small angles: n₁ i = n₂ r. Relate exterior angles to interior: i = α + γ, γ = r + β. Yields n₂/v - n₁/u = (n₂ - n₁)/R.

11. Lens Maker's Formula

Apply single spherical surface refraction to both faces of a thin lens and add equations to eliminate intermediate virtual image distance v₁.

12. Prism Refraction & Minimum Deviation

Geometric proof: A = r₁ + r₂ and δ = i + e - A. At minimum deviation D_m, i = e and r₁ = r₂ = A/2. Applying Snell's law yields μ = sin[(A+D_m)/2] / sin(A/2).

13. Huygens' Principle: Proof of Snell's Law

Consider incident plane wavefront AB on interface. Time taken to reach C is t = BC/v₁. Secondary wave from A travels distance AD = v₂t in medium 2. sin i / sin r = (BC/AC) / (AD/AC) = v₁/v₂ = n₂/n₁.

14. Young's Double Slit Fringe Width (β)

Path difference Δx = S₂P - S₁P = yd/D. For bright fringes yd/D = nλ ⟹ y_n = nλD/d. Fringe width β = y_{n+1} - y_n = λD/d.

15. Bohr Model: Radii & Energy Levels of Hydrogen

Equate Coulomb force to centripetal force: (1/4πε₀)(e²/r²) = mv²/r. Apply Bohr's quantization mvr = nh/(2π) to eliminate v and solve for r_n and E_n.
