📘 CBSE Class 10 Social Science (History) — Chapter 3: The Making of a Global World Blueprint

Pre-modern global interconnections, ancient Silk Routes linking Asia with Europe and Africa, food travel (potatoes, noodles), conquest and biological warfare (smallpox in Americas), the 19th-century global economy, and the Great Depression.

🐫 Silk Routes & Trade Networks Historic overland and maritime trade pathways connecting China, India, and Persia with the Mediterranean. Facilitated exchange of Chinese silk, Indian spices, pottery, and spread of Buddhism and Islam.
🥔 Food Travel & Columbian Exchange Introduction of American crops (potatoes, tomatoes, maize, chili) transformed European diets. Irish Potato Famine (1845–49) killed nearly one million people due to potato blight.
🦠 Biological Conquest of the Americas Spanish and Portuguese conquistadors conquered America not solely by conventional arms, but using deadly smallpox germs to which indigenous populations had no natural immunity.
📉 The Great Depression (1929) US stock market crash on Wall Street (October 1929) triggered worldwide economic collapse: agricultural overproduction, banking failures, soaring unemployment, and fall of global commodity prices.

📝 Sample Board Examination Problems & Official Solutions

Q1 (Silk Routes): What were the 'Silk Routes' that linked distant parts of the pre-modern world?

  • (A) Vibrant trade and cultural networks connecting Asia with Europe and Northern Africa
  • (B) Underground escape tunnels built during the Crusades
  • (C) Rail lines constructed by the British in China
  • (D) Maritime routes used exclusively for transporting raw silk from India to America
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (A) — Vibrant trade and cultural networks connecting Asia with Europe and Northern Africa

Explanation: The historic Silk Routes were a vast trans-continental network of overland caravan tracks and maritime sea lanes that knit together distant regions of Asia with Europe and North Africa from pre-Christian antiquity up to the sixteenth century. Named after lucrative Chinese silk exports that traversed these routes, they facilitated multi-directional global flows: Chinese ceramics, Indian spices, and textiles travelled westward in exchange for precious Roman gold and silver, while Buddhist, Christian, and Islamic missionaries travelled east.

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 History, Chapter 3, Section 1.1

Q2 (Introduction of New Crops): Which common food crops were introduced to Europe and Asia only after Christopher Columbus accidentally discovered the Americas?

  • (A) Wheat, rice, and barley
  • (B) Sugarcane, tea, and coffee
  • (C) Cotton, jute, and indigo
  • (D) Potatoes, soya, groundnuts, maize, tomatoes, and chillies
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (D) — Potatoes, soya, groundnuts, maize, tomatoes, and chillies

Explanation: A remarkable dimension of pre-modern globalization was the international diffusion of staple agricultural crops following Christopher Columbus's transatlantic voyages to the Americas in 1492. Many of our everyday foundational foods—including potatoes, soya, groundnuts, maize, tomatoes, chillies, and sweet potatoes—were completely unknown to Eurasian and African cuisines before Columbus. These crops were originally domesticated by Native American civilizations and transformed nutrition, population growth, and agriculture across Eurasia.

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 History, Chapter 3, Section 1.2
