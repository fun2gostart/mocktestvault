📘 CBSE Class 10 Social Science (History) — Chapter 4: The Age of Industrialisation Blueprint

Proto-industrialization before the factory system, arrival of steam power, labor conditions in Victorian Britain, the slow pace of mechanization, emergence of textile mills in colonial India, and marketing goods through advertisements.

🧵 Proto-Industrialization System Network of decentralized rural domestic manufacturing financed by urban merchants before the rise of centralized factories. Peasants and artisans produced textiles in cottages.
⚙️ Steam Power & Hand Labour Steam engines (James Watt/Matthew Boulton) were expensive and broke down frequently. In Victorian Britain, cheap abundant human labor made capitalists prefer hand labor for seasonal industries.
🏭 Early Indian Industrialists Pioneers like Dwarkanath Tagore, Dinshaw Petit, Jamsetjee Nusserwanjee Tata (TISCO 1912), and Seth Hukumchand established early cotton, jute, and steel industries amidst colonial restrictions.
🏷️ Advertisements & Calenders Manufacturers used labels with images of Indian gods (Krishna, Saraswati) and historical emperors on cloth bundles ('Made in Manchester') to inspire consumer trust and domestic purchase.

📝 Sample Board Examination Problems & Official Solutions

Q1 (Proto-Industrialisation Definition): What does the term 'proto-industrialisation' refer to?

  • (A) The phase of space exploration and computer automation
  • (B) The decline of agriculture and return to hunting-gathering
  • (C) The establishment of cottage craft shops inside royal palaces
  • (D) The phase of industrialisation before factories began to appear, characterized by large-scale decentralized production for international markets
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (D) — The phase of industrialisation before factories began to appear, characterized by large-scale decentralized production for international markets

Explanation: Historians designate the decentralized phase of commercial manufacturing that preceded the rise of modern urban factories as 'proto-industrialisation'. During the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, European town craft guilds strictly monopolized production and prices. Consequently, urban merchants travelled into rural countryside villages, supplying raw wool and flax to impoverished peasant cottages. Peasant families engaged in spinning, weaving, and dyeing at home, producing goods for international markets without urban factories.

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 History, Chapter 4, Section 1

Q2 (Merchant Movement to Countryside): Why did merchants from European towns begin moving to the countryside in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries?

  • (A) Towns were completely destroyed by volcanic eruptions
  • (B) Peasants in villages had invented steam engines
  • (C) Urban craft and trade guilds were powerful in towns and restricted the entry of new producers
  • (D) Merchants were legally banished from cities by royal decrees
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (C) — Urban craft and trade guilds were powerful in towns and restricted the entry of new producers

Explanation: In late eighteenth-century Britain, cotton emerged as the undisputed symbol of the new factory industrial age. The development of specialized mechanized spinning and weaving machinery triggered a spectacular explosion in cotton textile manufacturing. Raw cotton imports into Britain skyrocketed from 2.5 million pounds in 1760 to over 22 million pounds by 1787. Concentrating mechanized operations under a single factory roof allowed industrial mill owners to exercise rigorous oversight over labour discipline and product quality.

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 History, Chapter 4, Section 1
