📘 CBSE Class 10 Social Science (Geography) — Chapter 9: Agriculture Blueprint

Farming systems in India (primitive subsistence, intensive subsistence, commercial plantation), cropping seasons (Rabi, Kharif, Zaid), major food and commercial crops, and institutional agricultural reforms.

🌾 Indian Cropping Seasons Kharif: Sown with onset of monsoon (June-July), harvested Sept-Oct (Rice, Maize, Jowar, Cotton). Rabi: Sown in winter (Oct-Dec), harvested April-June (Wheat, Barley, Gram, Mustard). Zaid: Summer crops (Watermelon, Cucumber).
🍚 Major Food Crops Climatic Criteria Rice: Kharif crop requiring high temp (>25°C), high humidity, annual rainfall >100 cm. Wheat: Rabi crop requiring cool growing season, bright sunshine, 50-75 cm rainfall.
☕ Plantation & Commercial Crops Tea (beverage crop on well-drained fertile acidic soil, Assam), Coffee (Arabica variety from Yemen, Nilgiris), Cotton (Black soil, 210 frost-free days), Jute ('Golden fiber', high rainfall/floods).
🚜 Institutional & Tech Reforms Green Revolution, White Revolution (Operation Flood), Land consolidation (Chakbandi), Abolition of Zamindari, Kisan Credit Card (KCC), Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS), and MSP.

📝 Sample Board Examination Problems & Official Solutions

Q1 (Primitive Subsistence Jhumming): In the North-Eastern states of India (Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram, and Nagaland), primitive slash-and-burn subsistence farming is commonly known as:

  • (A) Jhumming
  • (B) Bewar
  • (C) Podu
  • (D) Pamlou
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (A) — Jhumming

Explanation: Primitive subsistence farming, commonly known as slash-and-burn cultivation, is practiced on small patches of cleared forest land using primitive tools (hoe, dao, and digging sticks) with family or community labor. In the northeastern states of India—Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram, and Nagaland—it is traditionally and widely referred to as 'Jhum' or 'Jhumming'. When soil fertility diminishes after a few seasons, farmers abandon the patch and clear a fresh plot, allowing nature to replenish fertility through natural vegetation cycles. (NCERT Geography Chapter 4, Section 1.1).

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography, Chapter 4, Section 1.1

Q2 (Slash and Burn Regional Names): Which of the following regional names of slash-and-burn farming in India is correctly matched?

  • (A) Bewar - Kerala, Khil - Tamil Nadu
  • (B) Jhumming - Punjab, Kuruwa - Gujarat
  • (C) Pama Dabi - Maharashtra, Bringa - Haryana
  • (D) Dipa - Bastar district (Chhattisgarh), Podu - Andhra Pradesh, Valre - Rajasthan
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (D) — Dipa - Bastar district (Chhattisgarh), Podu - Andhra Pradesh, Valre - Rajasthan

Explanation: Slash-and-burn agriculture is known by diverse localized vernacular names across the Indian subcontinent: 'Bewar' or 'Dahiya' in Madhya Pradesh; 'Podu' or 'Penda' in Andhra Pradesh; 'Pama Dabi' or 'Koman' in Odisha; 'Kumari' in the Western Ghats; 'Valre' or 'Waltre' in South-Eastern Rajasthan; 'Khil' in the Himalayan belt; 'Kuruwa' in Jharkhand; and 'Dipa' in the Bastar district of Chhattisgarh and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. Internationally, it is called 'Milpa' in Mexico and Central America and 'Roca' in Brazil. (NCERT Geography Chapter 4, Section 1.1).

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography, Chapter 4, Section 1.1
