📘 CBSE Class 10 Social Science (Geography) — Chapter 12: Lifelines of National Economy Blueprint

Transport networks (roadways, railways, pipelines, waterways, airways), communication networks, international trade as economic barometer, and tourism as a thriving modern industry.

🛣️ Roadways Classification Golden Quadrilateral Super Highways (linking Delhi-Kolkata-Chennai-Mumbai by NHAI), National Highways, State Highways, District Roads, Border Roads (constructed by BRO in northern frontiers).
🚆 Railways Network Advantages Principal mode of freight and passenger transportation in India. Dense network in northern plains due to level land, high population density, and rich agricultural resources.
⛽ Pipeline & Inland Waterway Networks Pipelines transport crude oil, petroleum products, and natural gas (HVJ pipeline 1,700 km). National Waterway No. 1 on Ganga river (Allahabad to Haldia, 1,620 km).
🚢 Major Sea Ports & International Trade Kandla/Deendayal Port (tidal port, Gujarat), Mumbai (biggest port), Marmagao (iron ore export), New Mangalore, Kochi (natural harbor), Chennai, Visakhapatnam (deepest landlocked).

📝 Sample Board Examination Problems & Official Solutions

Q1 (Lifelines of a Nation Concept): Efficient and rapid movement of goods, services, and people over space is a prerequisite for fast development. Hence, which sector is termed as the 'lifeline' of a national economy?

  • (A) Cottage handicraft weaving
  • (B) Transport and communication
  • (C) Subsistence agriculture
  • (D) Commercial advertising
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (B) — Transport and communication

Explanation: Transport, communication, and international trade are inextricably linked and function as the vital circulatory lifelines of a modern national economy. The movement of raw materials from resource hubs to factories, and finished products to consumer markets over geographical space, depends entirely on seamless multi-modal transportation networks (roadways, railways, waterways, airways, and pipelines). Coupled with modern telecommunications, they integrate disparate regional economies into a unified domestic and global market.

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography, Chapter 7, Section 1

Q2 (Roadways Advantage over Railways): Which of the following is a key operational advantage of roadways over railways?

  • (A) Roadways provide door-to-door service and the cost of loading/unloading is much lower
  • (B) Roadways can transport thousands of tonnes of iron ore in a single trip cheaper than trains
  • (C) Roadways do not require any construction or maintenance capital
  • (D) Roadways cannot be built on undulating hilly terrains
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (A) — Roadways provide door-to-door service and the cost of loading/unloading is much lower

Explanation: Road transport possesses distinct operational advantages over railways in India's logistics landscape. Road construction capital costs are significantly lower per kilometer than laying railway tracks. Furthermore, roadways provide flexible, end-to-end door-to-door delivery, which substantially cuts intermediate loading and unloading handling expenses. Unlike rigid rail networks, roads can easily negotiate steep terrain gradients, sharp curves, and undulating mountain topography across the Western Ghats and the Himalayas.

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography, Chapter 7, Section 1.1
