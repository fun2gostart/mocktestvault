📘 CBSE Class 10 Social Science (Economics) — Chapter 20: Money and Credit Blueprint

Money as medium of exchange, eliminating double coincidence of wants in barter, modern forms of money (currency, demand deposits, cheques), loan activities of banks, terms of credit, and Self-Help Groups (SHGs).

💵 Money Eliminates Barter Barter economy required double coincidence of wants (both parties must desire each other's goods). Money acts as universal intermediate medium of exchange, eliminating this constraint.
🏦 Modern Money & Bank Lending Currency notes authorized by RBI. Commercial banks keep ~15% deposits as cash reserve for depositors and extend remaining 85% as credit/loans at interest.
📝 Terms of Credit & Debt Trap Terms of credit: Interest rate, collateral (security asset like land, vehicle pledged to lender), documentation, and mode of repayment. High informal moneylender interest creates vicious debt trap.
🤝 Formal vs Informal & SHGs Formal sector (Banks, Cooperatives supervised by RBI with low interest); Informal sector (moneylenders, traders charging 36-60% interest). Self-Help Groups (15-20 women pooling savings).

📝 Sample Board Examination Problems & Official Solutions

Q1 (Double Coincidence of Wants): In a barter economy where goods are directly exchanged without the use of money, which essential condition must be satisfied?

  • (A) Double coincidence of wants (both parties must agree to sell and buy each other's commodities)
  • (B) A formal promissory guarantee certified by the village panchayat
  • (C) Gold coins deposited with an escrow magistrate
  • (D) A fixed exchange price list published by the state government
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (A) — Double coincidence of wants (both parties must agree to sell and buy each other's commodities)

Explanation: In an ancient barter economy where commodities are exchanged directly without any monetary medium, exchange is constrained by the 'double coincidence of wants'. This means that what one party desires to sell must coincide precisely with what the other party wishes to buy (e.g. a shoemaker needing wheat must find a wheat farmer wanting shoes). Because searching for matching reciprocal trading partners is exceedingly difficult, time-consuming, and inefficient, the introduction of money revolutionized commerce by eliminating this obstacle.

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics, Chapter 3, Section 3.1

Q2 (Money as Medium of Exchange): Why is money referred to as a 'medium of exchange'?

  • (A) Because it is minted exclusively from precious silver and gold bullion
  • (B) Because it acts as an intermediary instrument in the transactions and exchange of goods and services
  • (C) Because it can only be exchanged inside foreign diplomatic embassies
  • (D) Because it changes its face value automatically every month
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (B) — Because it acts as an intermediary instrument in the transactions and exchange of goods and services

Explanation: Money is termed a 'medium of exchange' because it acts as a universally accepted intermediary asset in the purchase and sale of goods, assets, and services. In a modern economy, a person holding money does not have to search for double coincidences; they can immediately purchase any commodity or service desired from any merchant. By decoupling the act of sale from the act of purchase, money solves the indivisibility and storage problems of barter, serving as a universal standard unit of account and store of economic value.

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics, Chapter 3, Section 3.1
