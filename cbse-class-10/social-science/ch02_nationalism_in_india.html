Detailed Solution Review

📘 CBSE Class 10 Social Science (History) — Chapter 2: Nationalism in India Blueprint

First World War impact, Mahatma Gandhi's Satyagraha (Champaran, Kheda, Ahmedabad), the Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh massacre (1919), the Non-Cooperation and Khilafat Movements, the Salt March (1930), and Civil Disobedience.

✊ Gandhi's Satyagraha Philosophy Truth and non-violence as active moral force. Champaran (1917, indigo farmers), Kheda (1917, crop failure tax remission), and Ahmedabad (1918, cotton mill workers wage hike).
⚠️ Rowlatt Act & Jallianwala Bagh Rowlatt Act (1919) authorized detention without trial. On 13 April 1919, General Dyer opened fire on peaceful gathering at Baisakhi fair in Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar, killing hundreds.
🚫 Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22) Boycott of foreign goods, government schools, and courts. Surrender of titles. Called off by Gandhi in Feb 1922 following violent clash at Chauri Chaura police post.
🧂 Dandi Salt March & Civil Disobedience 12 March 1930: Gandhi marched 240 miles from Sabarmati to Dandi with 78 volunteers, breaking salt law on 6 April. Unlike Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience involved active violation of colonial laws.

📝 Illustrative Practice Questions & Worked Explanations

Q1 (Gandhi's Return to India): In which month and year did Mahatma Gandhi return to India from South Africa?

  • (A) October 1914
  • (B) April 1916
  • (C) January 1915
  • (D) March 1917
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (C) — January 1915

Explanation: Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi returned to India on 9 January 1915 (a landmark date commemorated annually in modern India as Pravasi Bharatiya Divas) at the age of 45, after spending over two decades in South Africa. In South Africa, he had developed and perfected the novel method of mass mobilization and moral resistance known as 'Satyagraha' (holding firmly to truth through non-violent passive resistance) to fight racial segregation and discriminatory laws against the Indian immigrant community. Upon his arrival in India, on the advice of his political mentor Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Gandhi spent his first year travelling extensively across the country by third-class railway carriages to understand the ground reality of Indian masses. (NCERT Class 10 History Chapter 2, Section 1).

Topic reference (not item-level provenance): NCERT Class 10 History, Chapter 2, Section 1.1

Q2 (Champaran Satyagraha 1917): Mahatma Gandhi organized his first successful Satyagraha in India in 1917 at Champaran (Bihar) to support peasants against:

  • (A) High revenue demands during famine
  • (B) The oppressive indigo plantation system
  • (C) Low wages in cotton textile mills
  • (D) The British Salt Law monopoly
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (B) — The oppressive indigo plantation system

Explanation: After returning from South Africa in January 1915, Mahatma Gandhi successfully organized his very first Satyagraha campaign on Indian soil in 1917 at Champaran, Bihar. He was persuaded by local peasant Raj Kumar Shukla to visit Champaran and investigate the plight of sharecroppers forced under the oppressive British 'Tinkathia' system to plant indigo on 3/20th of their fertile land. Gandhi's non-violent moral resistance inspired the peasants, leading to the appointment of an official inquiry committee and the refund of illegally extorted dues. (NCERT History Chapter 2, Section 1.1).
