Detailed Solution Review

📘 CBSE Class 10 Social Science (Political Science) — Chapter 14: Federalism Blueprint

Theory and practice of federalism in India, 'Coming Together' vs 'Holding Together' federations, constitutional division of legislative powers (Union, State, Concurrent Lists), and the 1992 Panchayati Raj decentralization.

🏛️ Key Features of Federalism Two or more tiers of government; each tier has its own jurisdiction in legislation, taxation, and administration specified in constitution; courts act as umpire in disputes.
🤝 Coming vs Holding Together 'Coming Together' federations: Independent states pool sovereignty to form bigger unit (USA, Switzerland, Australia). 'Holding Together': Large country divides power between national and state units (India, Spain, Belgium).
📜 Three-Fold Legislative Distribution Union List (97 items: Defense, Foreign affairs, Banking - Parliament only); State List (66 items: Police, Trade, Agriculture - State legislature only); Concurrent List (47 items: Education, Forests - both, Union prevails on conflict).
🗳️ 1992 Decentralization (73rd/74th Amendment) Mandatory regular local elections, 33% reservation for women in all local bodies, State Election Commission created, State Finance Commission shares revenues with Panchayats and Municipalities.

📝 Illustrative Practice Questions & Worked Explanations

Q1 (Federalism Definition): Federalism is a system of government in which power is divided between:

  • (A) The military high command and police forces
  • (B) Different multinational corporations
  • (C) The head of state and judicial magistrates only
  • (D) A central authority and various constituent units of the country
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (D) — A central authority and various constituent units of the country

Explanation: Federalism is a constitutional system of government in which sovereign authority is legally divided between a central national authority and various regional constituent units (such as states or provinces). Usually, a federation has at least two levels of governance: the central government oversees subjects of common national interest (e.g. defence, currency), while state governments handle day-to-day administration within their territories. Both levels enjoy independent authority derived directly from the constitution.

Topic reference (not item-level provenance): NCERT Class 10 Civics, Chapter 2, Section 1

Q2 (Unitary vs Federal System): How does a federal system differ fundamentally from a unitary system of government?

  • (A) In a federal system, there are no regional courts or state police
  • (B) In a federal system, state governments have constitutional powers of their own for which they are not answerable to the central government
  • (C) In a unitary system, power is divided equally between 50 independent states
  • (D) In a federal system, the central government can dissolve state legislatures at its whim
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (B) — In a federal system, state governments have constitutional powers of their own for which they are not answerable to the central government

Explanation: In a unitary system of government (such as the UK or France), either there is only one central level of government or sub-units are subordinate agents whose powers can be withdrawn or commanded by the centre at will. In contrast, in a federal system (like India, USA, or Canada), state governments possess constitutionally guaranteed powers of their own. State governments are not mere delegates of the central executive and are not answerable to the centre for their state-list jurisdiction; both are separately accountable to citizens.

Topic reference (not item-level provenance): NCERT Class 10 Civics, Chapter 2, Section 1
