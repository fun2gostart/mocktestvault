📘 CBSE Class 10 Mathematics (Probability) — Chapter 14: Probability Blueprint

Classical theoretical probability of an event, elementary events, sample spaces (coins, dice, playing cards), impossible events (P = 0), sure events (P = 1), and complementary events P(E) + P(not E) = 1.

🎲 Classical Probability Formula P(E) = Number of outcomes favorable to E / Total number of possible outcomes = n(E) / n(S). Probability always satisfies: 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1.
🪙 Coins & Dice Sample Spaces Single coin: {H, T} (2). Two coins: {HH, HT, TH, TT} (4). Three coins: 8 outcomes. Single die: {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6} (6). Pair of dice: 6 × 6 = 36 outcomes.
🃏 Standard 52-Card Pack Breakdown 52 cards = 26 Red (13 Hearts, 13 Diamonds) + 26 Black (13 Spades, 13 Clubs). Face cards = 12 (4 Kings, 4 Queens, 4 Jacks). Aces = 4 (not face cards).
🚫 Impossible & Sure Events Impossible event: P(E) = 0 (e.g. rolling 7 on a standard 6-sided die). Sure/Certain event: P(E) = 1. Complementary relation: P(E) + P(Ē) = 1 ⇒ P(Ē) = 1 - P(E).

📝 Sample Board Examination Problems & Official Solutions

Q1 (Definition and Range of Probability): Which of the following numbers can never represent the probability of an event?

  • (A) 0.7
  • (B) 15%
  • (C) 2/3
  • (D) -1.5
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (D) — -1.5

Explanation: According to the fundamental axioms of probability in NCERT Class 10 Chapter 14, the probability P(E) of any empirical or theoretical event E is strictly bounded by 0 <= P(E) <= 1. Probability can never be a negative real number (such as -1.5) and can never strictly exceed 1 (or 100%). Any value outside the closed interval [0, 1] is mathematically invalid as a probability measure.

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Chapter 14 Probability, Section 14.1

Q2 (Complementary Events): If P(E) = 0.05, what is the probability of 'not E' (P(Ē))?

  • (A) 0.95
  • (B) 0.05
  • (C) 0.50
  • (D) 0.90
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (A) — 0.95

Explanation: The event E and its negation 'not E' (denoted as Ē) are mutually exclusive and exhaustive complementary events covering the complete sample space S. By the complementary probability theorem: P(E) + P(Ē) = 1. Given P(E) = 0.05, we compute: P(Ē) = 1 - P(E) = 1 - 0.05 = 0.95. Thus, the probability of the complementary event 'not E' occurring is precisely 0.95 (or 95%).

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Chapter 14 Probability, Section 14.1
