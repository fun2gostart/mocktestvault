Chapter 1

Real Numbers

Practice Ch 1 Mocks →
1. Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every composite number can be expressed (factorised) as a product of primes, and this factorisation is unique, apart from the order in which the prime factors occur.

2. HCF and LCM Product Rule
HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b

Applicable strictly to any two positive integers a and b. (Does NOT hold directly for three numbers: HCF(a,b,c) × LCM(a,b,c) ≠ a·b·c).

3. Decimal Expansion of Rational Numbers

For rational number p/q in simplest form (where HCF(p, q) = 1):

Terminating Decimal ⟺ q = 2^n × 5^m (where n, m are non-negative integers)

If prime factorisation of q contains any prime factor other than 2 or 5 (e.g., 3, 7, 11), the decimal expansion is non-terminating repeating (recurring).

Polynomials

Practice Ch 2 Mocks →
1. Quadratic Polynomial Zeroes & Coefficients

For standard quadratic polynomial p(x) = ax² + bx + c (a ≠ 0) with zeroes α and β:

Sum of Zeroes: α + β = -b / a = -(Coefficient of x) / (Coefficient of x²)

Product of Zeroes: α × β = c / a = (Constant term) / (Coefficient of x²)
2. Forming a Quadratic Polynomial Given Zeroes
p(x) = k [x² - (α + β)x + αβ] = k [x² - (Sum)x + (Product)]

Where k is any non-zero real constant.

3. Cubic Polynomial Relationships (Reference)

For p(x) = ax³ + bx² + cx + d with zeroes α, β, γ:

α + β + γ = -b/a | αβ + βγ + γα = c/a | αβγ = -d/a

Chapter 4

Quadratic Equations

Practice Ch 4 Mocks →
1. Standard Form & Discriminant
Standard Form: ax² + bx + c = 0 (a ≠ 0)

Discriminant: D = b² - 4ac
2. Nature of Roots (Discriminant Cases)

• If D > 0: Two distinct real roots.

• If D = 0: Two equal real roots (x = -b / 2a).

• If D < 0: No real roots (roots are imaginary / complex).

3. Quadratic Formula (Shreedharacharya's Rule)
x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / (2a) = [-b ± √D] / (2a)
Chapter 5

Arithmetic Progressions (AP)

Practice Ch 5 Mocks →
1. General Term (n-th Term) of an AP
aₙ = a + (n - 1)d

Where a is first term, d is common difference (d = a₂ - a₁ = aₖ - aₖ₋₁), and n is term position.

2. n-th Term from the End of an AP
aₙ (from end) = l - (n - 1)d

Where l is the last term of the finite arithmetic progression.

3. Sum of First n Terms of an AP
Sₙ = (n / 2) [2a + (n - 1)d] = (n / 2) [a + l]

Sum of first n natural numbers: Sₙ = n(n + 1) / 2

Relationship: aₙ = Sₙ - Sₙ₋₁
Chapter 6

Triangles

Practice Ch 6 Mocks →
1. Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales' Theorem 6.1)

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, the other two sides are divided in the same ratio:

In ΔABC, if DE ∥ BC, then: AD / DB = AE / EC
2. Criteria for Similarity of Triangles

• AAA (or AA) Similarity: If two angles of one triangle are respectively equal to two angles of another triangle, the triangles are similar.

• SSS Similarity: If the corresponding sides of two triangles are proportional, their corresponding angles are equal, and hence the triangles are similar.

• SAS Similarity: If one angle of a triangle is equal to one angle of another triangle and the sides including these angles are proportional, the triangles are similar.

Chapter 7

Coordinate Geometry

Practice Ch 7 Mocks →
1. Distance Formula
d = √[(x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²]

Distance from Origin (0, 0): d = √(x² + y²)
2. Section Formula (Internal Division)

Coordinates of point P(x, y) dividing line segment AB joining A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂) internally in ratio m₁ : m₂:

P(x, y) = [ (m₁x₂ + m₂x₁) / (m₁ + m₂), (m₁y₂ + m₂y₁) / (m₁ + m₂) ]
3. Midpoint Formula
M(x, y) = [ (x₁ + x₂) / 2, (y₁ + y₂) / 2 ]

cos θ 1 √3/2 1/√2 1/2 0

cot θ Not defined √3 1 1/√3 0
2. Fundamental Trigonometric Pythagorean Identities
sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 ⟺ sin²θ = 1 - cos²θ | cos²θ = 1 - sin²θ

1 + tan²θ = sec²θ ⟺ sec²θ - tan²θ = 1 | (sec θ - tan θ)(sec θ + tan θ) = 1

1 + cot²θ = csc²θ ⟺ csc²θ - cot²θ = 1 | (csc θ - cot θ)(csc θ + cot θ) = 1
Chapter 9

Some Applications of Trigonometry

Practice Ch 9 Mocks →
1. Angle of Elevation vs Angle of Depression

• Angle of Elevation: Angle formed by the line of sight with the horizontal when the point being viewed is above the horizontal plane.

• Angle of Depression: Angle formed by the line of sight with the horizontal when the point being viewed is below the horizontal plane.

Angle of Elevation from ground = Angle of Depression from top (Alternate Interior Angles)
2. Two-Position Tower Distance Formula

When moving a distance d towards a tower of height h changing angle from θ₁ to θ₂:

h = d / (cot θ₁ - cot θ₂)
Chapter 10

Circles

Practice Ch 10 Mocks →
1. Tangent-Radius Perpendicularity (Theorem 10.1)

The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact:

OP ⊥ XY at Point of Contact P
2. Lengths of Tangents from External Point (Theorem 10.2)

The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal:

PA = PB

Furthermore, tangents subtend equal angles at the center (∠AOP = ∠BOP) and are equally inclined to the line segment joining the center to that point (∠APO = ∠BPO).

Areas Related to Circles

Practice Ch 11 Mocks →
1. Circle Fundamentals
Circumference = 2πr = πd | Area = πr² = (π/4)d²
2. Sector Formulas (Angle θ in degrees)
Length of Arc (l) = (θ / 360°) × 2πr

Area of Sector = (θ / 360°) × πr² = (1/2) × l × r
3. Segment of a Circle
Area of Minor Segment = Area of Sector - Area of Corresponding ΔOAB

Area of ΔOAB with central angle θ: Area = (1/2) r² sin θ

Area of Major Segment = Total Circle Area - Area of Minor Segment

Chapter 13

Statistics

Practice Ch 13 Mocks →
1. Mean of Grouped Data
Direct Method: x̄ = (∑ fᵢxᵢ) / (∑ fᵢ)

Assumed Mean Method: x̄ = a + (∑ fᵢdᵢ) / (∑ fᵢ) [where dᵢ = xᵢ - a]
2. Mode of Grouped Data
Mode = l + [ (f₁ - f₀) / (2f₁ - f₀ - f₂) ] × h

Where l = lower limit of modal class, h = class size, f₁ = frequency of modal class, f₀ = frequency of preceding class, f₂ = frequency of succeeding class.

3. Median of Grouped Data
Median = l + [ (N/2 - cf) / f ] × h

Where l = lower limit of median class, N = total frequency, cf = cumulative frequency of preceding class, f = frequency of median class, h = class width.

4. Empirical Relationship Between Central Tendencies
3 × Median = Mode + 2 × Mean
Chapter 14

Probability

Practice Ch 14 Mocks →
1. Classical Probability Definition
P(E) = (Number of outcomes favourable to E) / (Total number of possible outcomes) = n(E) / n(S)
2. Probability Range & Complementary Events
0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1

P(E) + P(not E) = 1 ⟺ P(Ē) = 1 - P(E)

• Probability of an impossible event is 0.

• Probability of a sure (certain) event is 1.

What is the most important theorem in CBSE Class 10 Maths?
The Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales' Theorem 6.1) in Triangles and Theorem 10.2 (Lengths of tangents from an external point to a circle are equal) in Circles are statutory must-know theorems that are frequently asked for 3 to 5 marks in the CBSE Class 10 Board exam.
How can I memorize all Class 10 trigonometric formulas?
Remember the primary Pythagorean identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. Dividing both sides by cos²θ produces 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ; dividing both sides by sin²θ produces 1 + cot²θ = csc²θ. For values at 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°, memorize the sin row (0, 1/2, 1/√2, √3/2, 1), and simply reverse it for cos.
