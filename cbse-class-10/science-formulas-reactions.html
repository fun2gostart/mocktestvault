1. Fundamental Reaction Categories

Classification Definition & Governing Rule Standard NCERT Chemical Equations
Combination Reaction Two or more reactants combine to form a single product. Often highly exothermic. • CaO(s) + H2O(l) → Ca(OH)2(aq) + Heat (Slaking of lime)
• C(s) + O2(g) → CO2(g) (Coal combustion)
Decomposition Reaction A single reactant breaks down into two or more simpler products upon supplying energy (heat, light, or electricity). Always endothermic. • Thermal: CaCO3(s) → CaO(s) + CO2(g)
• Electrolytic: 2H2O(l) → 2H2↑ + O2↑ (Ratio 2:1 by volume)
• Photolytic: 2AgBr(s) → 2Ag(s) + Br2(g) (Black & white photography)
Displacement Reaction A more reactive chemical element displaces a less reactive element from its aqueous salt solution. • Zn(s) + CuSO4(aq) → ZnSO4(aq) + Cu(s)
• Pb(s) + CuCl2(aq) → PbCl2(aq) + Cu(s)
Double Displacement Mutual exchange of ions between two ionic compounds in aqueous solution forming a precipitate or neutral compound. • Na2SO4(aq) + BaCl2(aq) → BaSO4↓ + 2NaCl(aq)
Redox (Oxidation-Reduction) Simultaneous oxidation (loss of electrons / addition of O / removal of H) and reduction (gain of electrons / removal of O / addition of H). • MnO2 + 4HCl → MnCl2 + 2H2O + Cl2↑
(HCl is oxidized to Cl2; MnO2 is reduced to MnCl2)
Corrosion & Rancidity Formulas:
• Rusting of Iron: 4Fe + 3O2 + 2xH2O → 2Fe2O3·xH2O (Hydrated ferric oxide). Requires both oxygen and moisture.
• Corrosion of Copper: Forms a basic green carbonate coat CuCO3·Cu(OH)2.
• Tarnishing of Silver: Reacts with traces of H2S in air to form black Ag2S.
• Rancidity Prevention: Flushing oily foods with nitrogen (N2 unreactive gas) or adding antioxidants (BHA/BHT).

Reactant Combination General Rule Standard Balanced Chemical Equations

Metallurgical Extraction Reactions

Extraction Step Process Definition Chemical Reaction Equations
Roasting Heating sulfide ores below melting point in excess air to convert them to oxides. • 2ZnS(s) + 3O2(g) → 2ZnO(s) + 2SO2(g)↑
• 2Cu2S + 3O2 → 2Cu2O + 2SO2↑
Calcination Heating carbonate ores below melting point in limited or absence of air. • ZnCO3(s) → ZnO(s) + CO2(g)↑
Reduction with Carbon Heating metal oxide with carbon (coke) to yield free metal. • ZnO(s) + C(s) → Zn(s) + CO(g)↑
Thermit Reaction Reduction of metal oxide using highly reactive Aluminum powder. Extremely exothermic. • Fe2O3(s) + 2Al(s) → 2Fe(l) + Al2O3(s) + Heat
(Molten iron produced is used to join cracked railway tracks)

Essential Organic Reaction Mechanisms

Reaction Type Substrates & Catalyst Balanced Organic Equation & Key Note
Complete Combustion Hydrocarbon + Excess Oxygen CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O + Heat & Light
Saturated hydrocarbons burn with clean blue flame; unsaturated burn with sooty yellow smoke.
Controlled Oxidation Ethanol + Alkaline KMnO4 (or acidified K2Cr2O7) + Heat CH3CH2OH + 2[O] → CH3COOH + H2O
Oxidizing agents supply nascent oxygen to convert alcohol into carboxylic acid.
Addition (Hydrogenation) Unsaturated Vegetable Oil + H2 (Nickel / Palladium catalyst) R2C=CR2 + H2 → R2CH-CHR2
Converts liquid vegetable oils into solid vegetable ghee (vanaspati).
Substitution Methane + Chlorine in presence of Sunlight (hν) CH4 + Cl2 → CH3Cl + HCl
Chlorine atoms progressively replace hydrogen atoms one by one.
Esterification Ethanoic acid + Ethanol + Conc. H2SO4 (acid catalyst) CH3COOH + C2H5OH → CH3COOC2H5 + H2O
Produces Ethyl ethanoate (ester) characterized by a sweet, fruity odor.
Saponification Ester + Sodium hydroxide (alkaline hydrolysis) CH3COOC2H5 + NaOH → CH3COONa + C2H5OH
Sodium ethanoate formed is the basis of soap manufacturing.

🔍 Chapters 9 & 10: Light, Mirrors, Lenses & Human Eye

Physics Optics Master Formula Bank
🚀 Launch Chapter 9 Mock
Optical Formula Mathematical Equation Cartesian Sign Convention & Notes
Mirror Formula 1/f = 1/v + 1/u Concave mirror: f is negative (-); Convex mirror: f is positive (+). Object distance u is always negative.
Mirror Magnification m = hi / ho = -v / u If m is negative: Real & inverted image; If m is positive: Virtual & erect image.
Snell's Law of Refraction n21 = sin i / sin r = v1 / v2 Constant for given pair of media and color of light. Light bends towards normal when entering denser medium.
Absolute Refractive Index n = c / v c = 3.0 × 108 m/s (speed in vacuum). Optical density depends on speed of light, not mass density.
Lens Formula 1/f = 1/v - 1/u Convex lens (converging): f is positive (+); Concave lens (diverging): f is negative (-).
Lens Magnification m = hi / ho = +v / u Notice positive (+v/u) sign, unlike spherical mirrors which carry negative (-v/u).
Power of a Lens P = 1 / f (in meters) Unit: Dioptre (D). Convex lens has positive power (+D); Concave lens has negative power (-D). Combination: P = P1 + P2.

⚡ Chapter 11: Electricity & Joule's Heating

Current, Potential, Resistance & Power Formulas
🚀 Launch Chapter 11 Mock
Electrical Concept Formula Equation SI Units & Constants
Electric Current I = Q / t = n·e / t Ampere (A) = Coulomb / second. e = 1.6 × 10-19 C. 1 Coulomb ≈ 6.25 × 1018 electrons.
Potential Difference V = W / Q Volt (V) = Joule / Coulomb. Work done moving 1 C charge between two points.
Ohm's Law V = I·R At constant temperature, current is directly proportional to potential difference.
Resistivity Formula R = ρ · (l / A) ρ is resistivity in Ω·m. Independent of length and area; depends only on material and temperature.
Series Combination Rs = R1 + R2 + R3 Current I remains identical across all series resistors; total voltage divides: V = V1 + V2 + V3.
Parallel Combination 1/Rp = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3 Voltage V remains identical across all branches; equivalent resistance is lower than smallest individual resistor.
Joule's Law of Heating H = I2Rt = VIt = (V2/R)t Heat produced in Joules (J). Heating is directly proportional to square of current.
Electric Power P = VI = I2R = V2 / R Watt (W) = Volt × Ampere = J/s. Commercial unit: 1 kWh = 3.6 × 106 J.

🧲 Chapter 12: Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

Rules, Field Patterns & Domestic Circuits
🚀 Launch Chapter 12 Mock
👍 Right-Hand Thumb Rule Imagine holding a current-carrying straight wire with your right hand such that the thumb points along the current direction. The curled fingers encircle the wire in the direction of magnetic field lines (concentric circles).
✋ Fleming's Left-Hand Rule Stretch thumb, forefinger, and middle finger mutually perpendicular: Forefinger = Magnetic Field (B), Middle Finger = Current (I), Thumb = Motion / Mechanical Force (F). Principle of electric motors.
🔌 Domestic Circuit Specifications • Standard voltage: 220 V AC at 50 Hz.
• Live wire: Red / Brown insulation (+220 V).
• Neutral wire: Black / Blue insulation (0 V).
• Earth wire: Green / Yellow insulation (safety low resistance path).
