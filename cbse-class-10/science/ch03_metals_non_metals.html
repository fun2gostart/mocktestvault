Detailed Solution Review

📘 CBSE Class 10 Science (Chemistry) — Chapter 3: Metals and Non-Metals Blueprint

Details physical and chemical properties of metals and non-metals, ionic bond formation, the reactivity series, and industrial metallurgical processes for extracting metals from their mineral ores.

⚡ Reactivity Series & Properties K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > [H] > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au. Highly reactive metals (K, Na) react vigorously with cold water; Mg reacts with hot water; Fe, Al react with steam.
🔬 Amphoteric Oxides Metal oxides that react with both acids and bases to yield salt and water: Al2O3 + 6HCl -> 2AlCl3 + 3H2O and Al2O3 + 2NaOH -> 2NaAlO2 + H2O (Sodium aluminate).
💎 Ionic Bonding & Lattices Formed by complete transfer of valence electrons from metal to non-metal. Characteristics: high melting/boiling points, crystalline solids, soluble in water, conduct electricity in molten/aqueous state.
⛏️ Metallurgical Extraction Roasting (sulfide ores heated in excess air: 2ZnS + 3O2 -> 2ZnO + 2SO2), Calcination (carbonate ores in limited air: ZnCO3 -> ZnO + CO2), and Thermit welding (Fe2O3 + 2Al -> 2Fe + Al2O3).

📝 Illustrative Practice Questions & Worked Explanations

Q1 (Physical Properties of Metals): Which metal is liquid at room temperature, and which non-metal is a lustrous crystalline solid?

  • (A) Metal: Sodium; Non-metal: Sulphur
  • (B) Metal: Mercury; Non-metal: Iodine
  • (C) Metal: Gallium; Non-metal: Carbon
  • (D) Metal: Lead; Non-metal: Phosphorus
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (B) — Metal: Mercury; Non-metal: Iodine

Explanation: While metals are typically hard solids with high melting points, Mercury (Hg, atomic number 80) is the unique metal that exists as a liquid at standard room temperature (25°C, melting point -38.8°C). Conversely, non-metals are generally dull solids or gases with poor reflectance, but Iodine (I₂, atomic number 53) is an exceptional non-metal that forms dark purple-black lustrous crystalline plates with a distinct metallic sheen. Gallium and Caesium also have very low melting points (<30°C) and melt in the palm of a hand.

Topic reference (not item-level provenance): NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 3, Section 3.1

Q2 (Reactivity with Water): Metals like sodium and potassium react violently with cold water. Why are sodium and potassium stored submerged in kerosene oil?

  • (A) To prevent them from evaporating into air
  • (B) To dissolve their outer oxide coating
  • (C) To prevent vigorous exothermic reaction with atmospheric oxygen and moisture that catches fire
  • (D) To keep their metallic luster shiny for display
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (C) — To prevent vigorous exothermic reaction with atmospheric oxygen and moisture that catches fire

Explanation: Alkali metals like Sodium (Na) and Potassium (K) have very low ionization enthalpies and are positioned at the top of the reactivity series. When exposed to ambient air, they react violently with atmospheric moisture and oxygen: 2Na(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g) + Heat. The enthalpy released during this rapid exothermic reaction is sufficient to ignite the liberated dihydrogen gas (H₂), causing spontaneous combustion with a bright yellow/lilac flame. Immersing them in unreactive kerosene oil cuts off contact with air and water.

Topic reference (not item-level provenance): NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 3, Section 3.2.2
