📘 CBSE Class 10 Science (Physics) — Chapter 11: Electricity Blueprint

Fundamental physics of electric charge, potential difference, Ohm's law, electrical resistance and material resistivity, series and parallel circuit analysis, Joule's heating effect, and electrical power calculations.

⚡ Charge, Current & Potential Current I = Q / t = ne / t (Amperes). Potential difference V = W / Q (Volts). Ohm's Law: V = IR at constant temperature, yielding a linear V-I slope representing resistance R.
📏 Resistance & Material Resistivity R = ρ (l / A). Resistivity ρ (in Ω·m) depends solely on material and temperature. Stretching a wire to double its length quarters area, quadrupling resistance (R' = 4R).
🔌 Resistor Networks (Series vs Parallel) Series: Rs = R1 + R2 + R3 (same current I throughout). Parallel: 1/Rp = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3 (same voltage V across all branches, equivalent resistance is lower than smallest individual resistor).
🔥 Joule's Heating & Electric Power Heat H = I²Rt = VIt = (V²/R)t (Joules). Power P = VI = I²R = V²/R (Watts). Commercial electrical unit: 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ Joules = 1 Board of Trade Unit.

📝 Sample Board Examination Problems & Official Solutions

Q1 (Resistance: Uniform Wire Stretching): A cylindrical metal wire of resistance R is stretched uniformly so that its length becomes twice its original length (l' = 2l). Assuming its density and volume remain constant, what is the new resistance of the wire?

  • (A) 4R
  • (B) 2R
  • (C) R/2
  • (D) R/4
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (A) — 4R

Explanation: Because volume is conserved (V = A * l = constant), doubling the length (l' = 2l) causes the cross-sectional area to be halved (A' = A/2). New resistance R' = rho * l' / A' = rho * (2l) / (A/2) = 4 * (rho * l / A) = 4R.

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 11, Section 11.5 Factors on which the Resistance of a Conductor Depends

Q2 (Domestic Circuit Architecture: Parallel vs Series): Why are domestic household lighting circuits and appliances wired in parallel rather than in series?

  • (A) Parallel wiring increases overall circuit resistance to save power
  • (B) Each appliance receives the full mains potential difference (220 V) and operates independently if another fails
  • (C) In series, all appliances draw the exact same power regardless of their resistance
  • (D) Parallel circuits eliminate the need for an earth ground connection
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (B) — Each appliance receives the full mains potential difference (220 V) and operates independently if another fails

Explanation: In a parallel arrangement, each appliance is connected directly across the supply voltage (220 V) and has its own independent switch. If one appliance fuses or is turned off, other branches remain unaffected. Furthermore, total resistance is reduced, allowing each device to draw its required current.

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 11, Section 11.6.2 Resistors in Parallel
