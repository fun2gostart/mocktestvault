Detailed Solution Review

📘 CBSE Class 10 Science (Biology / Ecology) — Chapter 13: Our Environment Blueprint

Ecosystem components (biotic and abiotic), food chains, food webs, trophic levels, Lindeman's 10% energy transfer law, biological magnification of pesticides, and environmental challenges including ozone layer depletion and waste management.

🌿 Ecosystem & Trophic Levels Producers (autotrophs) -> Primary consumers (herbivores) -> Secondary consumers (carnivores) -> Tertiary consumers (apex predators). Energy flow in an ecosystem is strictly unidirectional.
🔟 Lindeman's 10% Energy Transfer Law Only ~10% of chemical energy stored in organic matter at one trophic level is transferred to the next higher level; 90% is lost as metabolic heat. Limits food chain length to 3-4 steps.
⚠️ Biological Magnification Progressive accumulation of non-biodegradable pesticides (e.g. DDT) in increasing concentrations along successive trophic levels, reaching maximum concentration in top consumers (humans).
🛡️ Ozone Depletion & Waste Management Stratospheric ozone (O3) absorbs solar UV-B radiation. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) release chlorine radicals that catalytically destroy ozone molecules. 1987 Montreal Protocol froze CFC production.

📝 Sample Board Examination Problems & Official Solutions

Q1 (Energy Flow: Solar Energy Capture Efficiency): What percentage of the total solar energy falling on the leaves of green terrestrial plants is captured and converted into chemical food energy via photosynthesis?

  • (A) About 1%
  • (B) About 10%
  • (C) About 50%
  • (D) Nearly 100%
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (A) — About 1%

Explanation: NCERT explicitly notes: The green plants in a terrestrial ecosystem capture about 1% of the energy of sunlight that falls on their leaves and convert it into food (chemical energy).

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 13, Section 13.1.1 Food Chains and Webs

Q2 (Energy Flow: 10% Trophic Transfer Law): According to Lindeman's 10% law of energy transfer, how much energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next higher trophic level?

  • (A) 50% is transferred to the next level
  • (B) 100% of the energy is conserved and transferred without loss
  • (C) Only 0.1% reaches the consumer
  • (D) An average of 10% of food energy is transferred, while roughly 90% is lost to metabolic processes and heat
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (D) — An average of 10% of food energy is transferred, while roughly 90% is lost to metabolic processes and heat

Explanation: An average of 10% of the food eaten is turned into the organism's own body and made available for the next level of consumers. Therefore, 10% can be taken as the average value for the amount of organic matter that reaches the next level.

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 13, Section 13.1.1 Food Chains and Webs
