Detailed Solution Review

📘 CBSE Class 10 Science (Chemistry) — Chapter 4: Carbon and Its Compounds Blueprint

Explores the versatile tetravalent nature of carbon, catenation, covalent bonding, homologous series, IUPAC nomenclature, and vital organic reactions including combustion, oxidation, addition, esterification, and saponification.

🔗 Catenation & Tetravalency Carbon forms 4 covalent bonds sharing electrons to achieve stable octet. High C-C bond energy enables long chains, branched structures, and rings (catenation).
📐 Homologous Series & Groups Alkanes (CnH2n+2), Alkenes (CnH2n), Alkynes (CnH2n-2). Successive members differ by -CH2- unit (14u mass). Functional groups: -OH (alcohol), -CHO (aldehyde), >C=O (ketone), -COOH (acid).
🔥 Key Organic Reactions Combustion (CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O), Oxidation of ethanol to ethanoic acid via alkaline KMnO4, Addition (hydrogenation of oils using Ni), Substitution (CH4 + Cl2 under sunlight).
🧼 Esterification & Soaps Ethanoic acid + Ethanol -> Ethyl ethanoate (fruity ester). Saponification: alkaline hydrolysis of ester yields soap (sodium carboxylate) and glycerol. Micelle cleaning mechanism.

📝 Sample Board Examination Problems & Official Solutions

Q1 (Covalent Bonding in Carbon): Carbon forms covalent bonds rather than ionic bonds because forming C⁴⁺ or C⁴⁻ ions is energetically unfavorable. Which of the following properties are characteristic of covalent carbon compounds?

  • (A) High melting points and conduct electricity in solid state
  • (B) High electrical conductivity in molten state
  • (C) Soluble exclusively in inorganic polar solvents
  • (D) Low melting/boiling points and are poor conductors of electricity
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (D) — Low melting/boiling points and are poor conductors of electricity

Explanation: Carbon (atomic number 6, electronic configuration 2, 4) cannot gain 4 electrons to form C⁴⁻ (energy barrier for 6 protons to hold 10 electrons) nor lose 4 electrons to form C⁴⁺ (immense ionization energy required). Instead, carbon shares its 4 valence electrons to form covalent bonds. Covalent molecules possess strong intramolecular covalent bonds but comparatively weak intermolecular van der Waals forces between molecules, resulting in low melting and boiling points. Because electrons are shared and no free ions exist, they are poor conductors.

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 4, Section 4.1

Q2 (Allotropes of Carbon): Which allotrope of carbon consists of carbon atoms arranged in layers of hexagonal arrays held by weak van der Waals forces, making it soft, slippery, and a good conductor of electricity?

  • (A) Graphite
  • (B) Diamond
  • (C) Buckminsterfullerene (C-60)
  • (D) Carbon nanotube
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (A) — Graphite

Explanation: Graphite is a crystalline allotrope of carbon in which each carbon atom is covalently bonded to three neighbouring carbon atoms in the same plane, forming flat sheets of hexagonal arrays. Because only 3 of carbon's 4 valence electrons participate in localized sigma bonds, the fourth valence electron is delocalized throughout the planar pi-system, allowing graphite to conduct electricity exceptionally well. The hexagonal planes are held together by weak van der Waals forces, allowing them to slide smoothly, making graphite soft and slippery.

NCERT Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 4, Section 4.1.1 (Allotropes Box)
