Detailed Solution Review

📘 CBSE Class 10 Science (Biology) — Chapter 8: Heredity Blueprint

Explores transmission of genetic traits from parents to offspring, Gregor Mendel's classic experiments on garden peas (Pisum sativum), laws of segregation and independent assortment, and chromosomal sex determination in humans.

🌱 Mendel's Monohybrid Cross Cross of pure tall (TT) and dwarf (tt) plants yields 100% tall (Tt) in F1. Selfing F1 gives F2 phenotypic ratio 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf (3:1) and genotypic ratio 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt (1:2:1).
🌿 Mendel's Dihybrid Cross Cross of round yellow (RRYY) and wrinkled green (rryy) seeds yields F1 RrYy. F2 generation displays 9:3:3:1 phenotypic ratio, demonstrating Law of Independent Assortment.
🧬 Dominant vs Recessive Alleles Dominant allele expresses itself in both homozygous (TT) and heterozygous (Tt) states; recessive allele expresses only in homozygous condition (tt).
👶 Sex Determination in Humans Females possess homomorphic XX chromosomes (produce only X ova); males possess heteromorphic XY chromosomes (produce 50% X and 50% Y sperm). Father's sperm determines child's sex.

📝 Illustrative Practice Questions & Worked Explanations

Q1 (Mendelian Monohybrid Cross F2 Ratio): In a classical Mendelian monohybrid cross between a homozygous pure tall pea plant (TT) and a homozygous pure short dwarf plant (tt), what is the phenotypic ratio obtained in the F2 generation?

  • (A) 1 Tall : 1 Short
  • (B) 9 Tall : 3 Short : 3 Wrinkled : 1 Round
  • (C) 3 Tall : 1 Short
  • (D) All Tall (100%)
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (C) — 3 Tall : 1 Short

Explanation: F1 generation (Tt) self-pollinates (Tt × Tt). The resulting F2 offspring are TT (Tall), Tt (Tall), Tt (Tall), and tt (Short), yielding a phenotypic ratio of 3 Tall : 1 Short (genotypic ratio 1 : 2 : 1).

Topic reference (not item-level provenance): NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 8, Section 8.2

Q2 (Sex Determination in Humans): In human beings, how is the biological sex of a newborn child genetically determined?

  • (A) Entirely by the mother's X chromosome
  • (B) By the ambient temperature during incubation
  • (C) By the nutritional health of the placenta
  • (D) By whether the father's fertilizing sperm cell carries an X or a Y chromosome
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (D) — By whether the father's fertilizing sperm cell carries an X or a Y chromosome

Explanation: Females produce eggs containing only X chromosomes (22 + X). Males produce two types of sperms in equal 50:50 ratio: (22 + X) and (22 + Y). Fertilization by an X-sperm gives a female child (XX), and by a Y-sperm gives a male child (XY).

Topic reference (not item-level provenance): NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 8, Section 8.2.4
