Detailed Solution Review

📘 CBSE Class 10 Science (Biology) — Chapter 5: Life Processes Blueprint

Comprehensive coverage of vital physiological processes across autotrophic and heterotrophic nutrition, cellular respiration, human double circulation, plant vascular transport (xylem/phloem), and renal excretion.

🌿 Photosynthesis & Nutrition 6CO2 + 12H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6O2 + 6H2O in presence of chlorophyll and sunlight. Site: Chloroplast thylakoids and stroma. Stomatal opening regulated by turgid guard cells.
🫁 Respiration & ATP Pathways Glycolysis in cytoplasm converts glucose to pyruvate. Aerobic respiration in mitochondria yields 36-38 ATP; anaerobic fermentation yields ethanol + CO2 (yeast) or lactic acid (muscle fatigue).
❤️ Double Circulation System Heart pumps deoxygenated blood to lungs (pulmonary circuit) and oxygenated blood to body tissues (systemic circuit). Prevents mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood for high metabolic efficiency.
🩺 Nephron Excretion & Filtration Bowman's capsule filters blood under pressure (ultrafiltration). Selective reabsorption of glucose, amino acids, and water occurs along the renal tubule, concentrating urea into urine.

📝 Illustrative Practice Questions & Worked Explanations

Q1 (Photosynthesis Major Events): During the process of photosynthesis in green plants, which of the following events does NOT take place?

  • (A) Absorption of light energy by chlorophyll
  • (B) Conversion of light energy to chemical energy and splitting of water into hydrogen and oxygen
  • (C) Oxidation of carbon dioxide to form carbohydrates
  • (D) Reduction of carbon dioxide to form carbohydrates
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (C) — Oxidation of carbon dioxide to form carbohydrates

Explanation: Photosynthesis in green autotrophic plants proceeds through three fundamental biophysical stages: (1) Absorption of light energy by chlorophyll pigments located in chloroplast thylakoids; (2) Conversion of light energy into chemical energy (ATP and NADPH) accompanied by the photolysis (splitting) of water molecules: 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂↑; (3) Biochemical REDUCTION (not oxidation) of carbon dioxide to carbohydrates (glucose): 6CO₂ + 12H₂O —[Light/Chlorophyll]→ C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ + 6H₂O. Carbon dioxide is reduced by adding hydrogen from water.

Topic reference (not item-level provenance): NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 5, Section 5.2.1

Q2 (Respiration Pathways): When energy is required rapidly during intense muscular exercise in humans, pyruvate is converted into which compound in muscle cells, causing muscle fatigue and cramps?

  • (A) Ethanol + Carbon dioxide + Energy
  • (B) Lactic acid + Energy
  • (C) Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy
  • (D) Glucose + Pyruvate
🔍 View Correct Answer & Step-by-Step Marking Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (B) — Lactic acid + Energy

Explanation: Under normal resting conditions, human cells undergo aerobic cellular respiration inside mitochondria, fully oxidizing pyruvate to carbon dioxide, water, and 36-38 ATP molecules. However, during vigorous sprinting or intense muscular exertion, oxygen delivery via blood cannot keep pace with the cellular metabolic demand. In this hypoxic state, muscle cells resort to anaerobic lactic acid fermentation in the cytoplasm: Pyruvate (3-carbon) —[LDH]→ Lactic acid (3-carbon) + 2 ATP. Accumulation of lactic acid drops muscle pH, causing cramps.

Topic reference (not item-level provenance): NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 5, Section 5.3 (Breakdown of Glucose Diagram)
